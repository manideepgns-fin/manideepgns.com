## LinkedIn

My cat app once used 12% of the CPU while doing nothing.

It is called Cataholic. It puts a chunky cat on your Mac. When the cat is just sitting there, the app has no work to do.

But 12% of the CPU was gone anyway. The machine was busy being busy.

"No theatrics," I said.

Now it uses almost none.

I like this rule because it applies far outside a cat app. If something is idle, it should be idle. A process that looks active while producing nothing costs something, and the cost is easy to miss because nothing visibly breaks.

It is also a good check on a finished thing. Does it do work when there is work, and rest when there is none?

The cat passes now.
